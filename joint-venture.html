The Basics

What Is a Joint Venture?

A Joint Venture is a development arrangement in which a landowner contributes the property, or agreed development rights, and a development partner takes on agreed development responsibilities. The commercial benefits are then shared according to terms both parties agree to.

It allows a landowner to take part in the development of their property without having to undertake the entire development process on their own. What each party contributes, and what each party receives, is set out in the Joint Venture agreement.

  1. LandownerOwns the property
  2. Land / Agreed Development RightsContributed to the project
  3. Joint Venture AgreementSets out terms and responsibilities
  4. Development PartnerUndertakes agreed responsibilities
  5. Project DevelopmentPlanning, approvals and construction
  6. Agreed Entitlement / BenefitDelivered as per the agreement
Why Consider a Joint Venture?

Your Land Can Do More Than Simply Remain an Asset

Not every landowner wants an outright sale. Some would rather take part in what their land can become, through a development arrangement shaped around their own priorities.

What is possible depends on the property, the project and the terms agreed. Returns and appreciation are not guaranteed.

Depending on the arrangement, a Joint Venture may allow you to:

  • Retain developed property
  • Receive an agreed share of developed units or area
  • Partially monetise your entitlement
  • Combine property entitlement with financial consideration
  • Hold developed units as a long-term investment
  • Work with a development partner instead of developing on your own
  • Explore redevelopment of an existing property
Flexible Structures

Your Land. Your Objective. Your Structure.

There is no one-size-fits-all Joint Venture structure.

Every landowner starts with a different objective. Depending on the project structure, feasibility and mutually agreed terms, the arrangement can be shaped in different ways.

A

Retain Developed Property

Receive your entitlement as developed units or area and keep them, for your family's use or for the future.

B

Partial Monetisation

Retain part of your entitlement and sell another part, where the project structure allows it.

C

Property + Financial Consideration

A combination of developed property and a financial component, subject to the terms agreed with the development partner.

D

Long-Term Holding

Hold developed units as a long-term asset, for your own use or for rental, depending on your plans.

E

Redevelopment

For older buildings or under-used properties, explore whether redevelopment is feasible and what it could involve.

F

Custom Project Structure

Where several owners, larger parcels or specific objectives are involved, the structure can be tailored to the project, subject to feasibility.

Not every structure is available for every property. What is possible depends on feasibility and the terms agreed between the parties.

Roles in a Joint Venture

Commercial Terms

Commercial Structures Are Project-Specific

How the benefits of a Joint Venture are shared is decided project by project. Common approaches include:

A

Development / Area / Unit Share

The landowner receives an agreed share of the developed area or units, and the development partner retains the rest.

B

Revenue Share

The parties share the sale proceeds of the project in agreed proportions, instead of dividing the built area.

C

Property + Financial Consideration

The landowner receives developed property along with an agreed financial component.

D

Hybrid Structure

A combination of the above, shaped around the property and the objectives of both parties.

Commercial terms depend on factors such as

  • Land value
  • Location
  • Land extent
  • Development potential
  • Project size
  • Construction cost
  • Funding requirements
  • Development risk
  • Responsibilities of each party
  • Market conditions
  • Negotiation between the parties

There is no standard sharing ratio. Any ratio is arrived at through feasibility and negotiation, and is set out in the final agreement.

Many properties are held by families or by several co-owners. In such cases, a few additional points need to be addressed as discussions move forward:

Due Diligence

Before Development Comes Verification

Before any development commitment is made, the property needs to be reviewed carefully. This typically covers:

  • Title and ownership
  • Encumbrances
  • Survey and measurements
  • Access
  • Existing structures
  • Existing occupants or tenants, where applicable
  • Planning and development parameters
  • Existing liabilities
  • Other project-specific matters

Aventrix Realty facilitates coordination. Legal, title, technical, tax and statutory matters should be independently reviewed by appropriately qualified professionals.
